## Real Aircraft & The Flying Tigers Legacy

Before the United States formally entered World War II following Pearl Harbor, retired U.S. Army Air Corps Captain Claire Lee Chennault organized a covert group of volunteer aviators and ground crew to defend China's Burma Road lifeline against Imperial Japanese aggression. Authorized under secret presidential directive by Franklin D. Roosevelt, the unit was designated the **1st American Volunteer Group (AVG)**, forever etched into history as the **Flying Tigers**.

Operating 100 Curtiss Hawk 81A-2 fighters diverted from British RAF orders, the AVG painted ferociously grinning **shark mouths** beneath the chin radiators—a design inspired by RAF No. 112 Squadron in North Africa. Between December 1941 and July 1942, the Flying Tigers claimed approximately 297 destroyed enemy aircraft while losing only 14 pilots in combat, establishing a kill-to-loss ratio that became an enduring legend of fighter aviation.

This specific aircraft, **White 47**, was assigned to **Flight Leader John R. "Johnny" Petach Jr.** of the **2nd Pursuit Squadron ('Panda Bears')**. Petach scored multiple air-to-air victories over Japanese bombers. On July 10, 1942, during a dive-bombing mission over Linchuan, his aircraft was hit by anti-aircraft artillery; Petach gave his life in action and was posthumously awarded the Distinguished Service Cross.
