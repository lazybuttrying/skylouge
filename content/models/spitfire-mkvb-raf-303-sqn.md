## Real Aircraft & Squadron History

Following the Nazi invasion of Poland in September 1939, thousands of seasoned Polish Air Force personnel refused surrender, undertaking daring escapes through Romania, Hungary, and France. When France fell in June 1940, Polish aviators evacuated to Great Britain.

Facing relentless Luftwaffe blitzes during the Battle of Britain, the RAF High Command initially hesitated to integrate non-English speaking foreign pilots. However, facing critical pilot shortages, the Air Ministry authorized independent Polish units under RAF Fighter Command. Among them, **No. 303 'Kościuszko' Polish Fighter Squadron** quickly emerged as legendary. Officially credited with 126 aerial victories during the Battle of Britain, No. 303 became the highest-scoring Allied squadron of the campaign.

This specific airframe, **Spitfire Mk.Vb serial EN951 (coded RF-D)**, was the personal mount of **Squadron Leader Jan Zumbach**, one of Poland's most celebrated wartime aces with 13 confirmed kills, 5 probables, and 1 damaged. Beneath the cockpit windshield, Zumbach had painted his personal emblem: a fierce **Donald Duck wielding a wooden club**, a badge of defiance that became an icon of Polish fighter heritage.
